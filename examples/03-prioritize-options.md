# Prioritize Options

## Situation

A product team has four possible investments but only enough capacity for two. Senior stakeholders favor different options.

## Inputs

- four normalized options;
- decision criteria and their definitions;
- evidence behind impact, cost, time, dependency, and reversibility estimates;
- non-negotiable constraints;
- consequences of delay.

## Selected Methods

| Method | Why it changes the decision | Intermediate output | Effect on next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| `decision-matrix` | Makes criteria and tradeoffs explicit. | Scored comparison with evidence and sensitivity notes. | Produces a defensible provisional ranking. |
| `risk-matrix` | Prevents a high score from hiding unacceptable downside. | Risk exposure, controls, and escalation thresholds. | Converts ranking into a conditional decision. |

## Methods Not Used

- Do not use `rice-scoring`: reach and product-effort data are not comparable across all four options.
- Do not use `wsjf-prioritization`: cost-of-delay inputs are too weak to support economic sequencing.

## Decision Artifact

Select Options B and D. Option B remains first under all tested weight changes. Option D ranks second only if the regulatory dependency is cleared by the agreed date; otherwise select Option A. The decision records evidence strength and the trigger for changing the ranking.
