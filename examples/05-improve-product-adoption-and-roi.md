# Improve Product Adoption And ROI

## Situation

A digital product has low monthly usage and an uncertain return on investment. Stakeholder interviews contain many proposed features but little agreement on why target users do not adopt it.

## Inputs

- usage by role, cohort, workflow, and frequency;
- interview notes from users, product managers, and process owners;
- current costs and expected value mechanism;
- alternatives and manual workarounds;
- known data-quality gaps.

## Selected Methods

| Method | Why it changes the decision | Intermediate output | Effect on next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| `evidence-map` | Separates observed behavior from feature requests and explanations. | Adoption evidence and assumption ledger. | Defines what can be diagnosed now and what requires research. |
| `jobs-to-be-done` | Identifies the progress users seek and the forces blocking change. | Job statement, context, alternatives, anxieties, and success criteria. | Focuses intervention on the workflow rather than feature volume. |
| `metrics-tree` | Connects usage behavior to value and ROI. | Outcome, driver, behavior, and guardrail tree. | Creates measurable hypotheses and leading indicators. |

## Methods Not Used

- Do not use `kano-model`: satisfaction-feature classification does not yet explain non-adoption.
- Do not use `service-blueprint`: backstage service redesign is premature before the adoption mechanism is clear.

## Decision Artifact

The first intervention is not a new dashboard. It is an embedded approval workflow for one high-frequency user segment. Validate with activation-to-completion conversion, repeat use within 30 days, hours of manual coordination avoided, and a guardrail for approval errors.
