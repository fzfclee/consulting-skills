# Assess A Market Entry

## Situation

A mid-sized service company is considering entry into a new national market with uncertain regulation, established competitors, and several possible target segments.

## Inputs

- market boundary and time horizon;
- regulatory and economic evidence;
- competitor and substitute data;
- customer segments and buying criteria;
- internal capabilities, constraints, and economic assumptions.

## Selected Methods

| Method | Why it changes the decision | Intermediate output | Effect on next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pestel-analysis` | Identifies external conditions that can invalidate the entry thesis. | Evidence-backed external factor map. | Defines regulatory and timing gates. |
| `porter-five-forces` | Tests structural attractiveness rather than relying on market growth. | Force strength and profit-pressure diagnosis. | Identifies where bargaining power and substitutes threaten economics. |
| `competitive-positioning` | Tests whether the company has a defensible position for a chosen segment. | Segment, alternative, differentiator, proof, and tradeoff statement. | Converts market interest into a focused entry option. |

## Methods Not Used

- Do not use `swot-analysis`: the three selected methods already cover the decision-critical external and positioning questions.
- Do not use `business-model-canvas`: operating-model design follows only after the entry thesis passes its gates.

## Decision Artifact

Do not approve a broad national launch. Test one regulated segment through a local partner, conditional on licensing confirmation, a minimum gross-margin threshold, and evidence that the proposed service difference changes buyer choice.
